# Factual notes and source boundaries

## What the video says

- A 2017 article indexed by GARUDA, published in *Jurnal Bahasa, Sastra dan Pembelajarannya*, describes the Kingdom of Pulau Halimun legend as Kotabaru folklore. Its abstract connects Sambu Ranjana with Mount Saranjana and Sambu Batung with Mount Sebatung.
- The official BIPA/Badan Bahasa 2016 entry for *Asal Mula Kotabaru* states that the book records one of several versions of the Kotabaru origin legend. The library synopsis describes a giant and La Ode; this episode does not blend that distinct plot into the Saranjana legend.
- A 2023 detik feature summarizes historical and mythic interpretations, including a telling about the two sons and the unseen city. This is secondary context, not evidence that a supernatural city exists.

## Editorial limits

- Saranjana is presented as folklore and local myth. No supernatural event, eyewitness, sighting, or physical evidence is invented.
- Characters, old-map transitions, lights, shadows, and city imagery are visual reconstruction, not authentic archival footage or proof.
- Versions of oral tradition can differ. The narration avoids claiming there is only one canonical version.
